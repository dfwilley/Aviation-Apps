How grading works

On the ship, the Landing Signal Officer (LSO) watches every approach and writes a grade and a short comment. The squadron posts the grades as colored squares on the "greenie board". This app does the same job from the HXr recording.

What it measures. The recording has GPS position four times a second and pressure altitude, airspeed and bank about fifteen times a second. Touchdown is the moment the descent stops over a runway. Height is pressure altitude compared with the altitude at touchdown, so the altimeter setting does not matter.

Where it looks (distance before the runway threshold):

CodeMeaningWhere
XAt the start4,500 to 3,000 ft
IMIn the middle3,000 to 1,500 ft
ICIn close1,500 to 300 ft
ARAt the rampCrossing the threshold

A part is only judged if you were already lined up with the runway there (within 15 degrees and 400 ft).

What it checks.

  • Glidepath (H high, LO low): your angle to the target touchdown point against the target glidepath. Off by more than 0.5°, 1° and 2°.
  • Lineup (LUL left, LUR right): feet from the runway centerline. 60 ft at the start, 40 in the middle, 25 in close, 20 at the threshold; double that is a full comment.
  • Speed (F fast, SLO slow): in close and at the threshold only. Fast by 5, 10, 15 knots; slow by 4, 7, 10.
  • Touchdown: SHORT or LONG against your target point, LL or LR for left or right of the centerline, LLWD or LRWD for a wing down, and HARD from the G reading.
  • The groove: NESA is less than 12 seconds lined up (not enough straightaway). LIG is more than 35 seconds (long in the groove).

Reading the comment. (H) in brackets is a little. H alone is a clear deviation. H underlined is way off. So "(HX) FIC (LONG)" reads: a little high at the start, fast in close, touched down a little long.

Touchdown point. From 150 ft short of your target to 200 ft past it is on target. Shorter than that is (SHORT). Up to 500 ft long is (LONG), up to 1,000 ft is LONG, and more than 1,000 ft long is a bolter.

The grade. Small comments add up. A clear deviation in close, at the threshold or at touchdown makes the pass Fair. Anything way off close in, or touching down before the threshold, is No grade. A touchdown of 2 G or more is a Cut pass. No comments at all, with the touchdown on target, earns the underlined OK.

Day and night. A day landing is a full box in the grade color. A night landing is a box cut corner to corner, with the grade color above and black below. Night here is the FAA meaning: the sun more than 6° below the horizon at the airport (after evening civil twilight, before morning civil twilight). Ready rooms each marked night their own way; this is one of the ways.

OK (4.0), underlined OK is perfect (5.0) Fair (3.0) Bolter, very long (2.5) No grade (2.0) Cut pass (0.0) Full box: day Cut corner to corner with black: night

Limits. The published runway positions are good to about 20 ft, so do not read much into a few feet of lineup. The HXr smooths its G reading, so a firm landing may not show. If a recording stops before the wheels touch, that landing cannot be graded and the app says so.
